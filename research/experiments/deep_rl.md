# Deep RL runs for "Trial and Reward" — Modules 4–5 (episodes 12–19)

Measured on this Mac Studio (Apple M4 Max, 64 GB), 2026-10-25. Machine-readable twin: `deep_rl.json`
(stable keys; `headline` holds the numbers scenes assert). Everything here is regenerated by the commands below.

## Setup

- **Environments:** gymnasium 1.4.0 `CartPole-v1` (reward +1 per step, episode capped at 500 steps;
  *solved* = mean return of the last 100 training episodes ≥ 475, the environment's registered threshold) and
  `Pendulum-v1` (200 steps, reward between about −16 and 0 per step; −150 to −200 is a good swing-up).
- **Framework: numpy** (Python 3.12.14, numpy 2.5.4), a small MLP with hand-written backprop and Adam,
  gradient-checked against finite differences (`logs/deep_gradcheck.json`, max relative error < 1e-8). Why: for
  CartPole-sized networks (4-64-64-2, batch 64) one numpy training step took 76 µs vs
  133 µs in PyTorch CPU (one thread) and 208 µs in MLX
  (CPU); choosing one action took 4.8 µs vs 10.2 µs (PyTorch) and
  26.7 µs / 135 µs (MLX CPU / GPU). Tiny networks are all overhead, and numpy has
  the least. At SAC's 256-wide networks PyTorch was faster per update (278 vs
  425 µs), but a whole SAC run takes under a minute, so we kept one framework (the same one as
  `rl_reference.py`). The GPU was left to the language-model job. (`logs/deep_bench.json`)
- **Seeds:** hyperparameters were picked in pilot runs on seeds 0–5 (`logs/pilot/`); every reported number comes from
  **fresh seeds 10–14**, five per method, each run once, none dropped. Spread is the sample standard deviation across
  the five seeds, with min–max in brackets.
- **Wall time:** each seed is one single-threaded process; ten ran at once while other jobs shared the machine, so
  wall times are honest but a little pessimistic for a quiet Mac.
- **Commands** (from `research/experiments/`):
  ```
  uv venv .venv-deep --python 3.12 && VIRTUAL_ENV=.venv-deep uv pip install gymnasium numpy torch mlx
  .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_bench.py         # framework choice
  .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_gradcheck.py     # gradients are right
  nohup .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_run_all.py 10 > logs/deep_run_all.out &   # all 65 runs, ~5 min
  .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_gradvar.py       # gradient variance (ep 17)
  .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_export.py        # weights/*.npz + trajectories.json
  .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_aggregate.py     # deep_rl.json
  .venv-deep/bin/python scripts/deep_report.py        # this file
  ```

## Episode 13 — DQN and what its two tricks buy

Network 4-256-256-2 (ReLU), Adam 2.5e-4, Huber loss, γ = 0.99, replay buffer 100k, batch 128, one gradient step every 4
env steps, target network copied every 1,000 steps, ε from 1 to 0.01 over the first 20k steps, 500k steps per run.

- **DQN solved CartPole in all 5 seeds**, after 252k env steps on average
  (145k–446k), about 1,376 episodes
  and 35 s of wall time (70 s for the whole 500k-step run).
- **It does not stay solved.** 2 of 5 seeds later fell below an average of 300;
  at the end of the budget the mean of the last 100 episodes was 283 ± 184.
  DQN's famous instability ("catastrophic forgetting") is real even on CartPole — show the wobbly curves, not just the peak.
- **No replay (one update on the newest transition each step): 0 of 5 solved.** Best 100-episode average
  157 ± 14, ending at
  61. Its Q-values drifted to ~202,
  twice the largest value any CartPole policy can have (100).
- **No target network (bootstrap from the network being trained): 0 of 5 solved — it diverged.** Q-values grew without
  bound (≈ 4.1e+09 after 500k steps) and the policy collapsed to pushing one way
  (episodes of ~9 steps, best 100-episode average 22). This is the "deadly triad"
  of episode 12 — function approximation + bootstrapping + off-policy data — happening for real.

## Episode 14 — overestimation, measured

Every 5,000 steps the greedy policy played the same 10 start states. We logged the network's own prediction
max_a Q(s0, a) and the discounted return (γ = 0.99) it then actually collected. No CartPole policy can be worth more than
1/(1−γ) = 100 (a full 500-step episode collects 99.34).

| (evaluations after 300k steps, 5 seeds) | DQN | Double DQN |
|---|---|---|
| predicted Q(s0, a*) | 102.8 | 99.5 |
| discounted return actually achieved | 94.0 | 97.8 |
| overestimate (predicted − achieved) | **+8.8** | **+1.7** |
| … on start states where the episode lasted all 500 steps | +1.89 | +0.09 |
| share of predictions above the impossible ceiling of 100 | 79% | 34% |
| peak predicted Q(s0) during training (mean of seeds' peaks) | 110.8 | 101.7 |
| mean Q on training batches at the end | 112.7 | 101.9 |
| solved (seeds) / steps to solve | 5/5 / 252k ± 118k | 5/5 / 175k ± 40k |
| final avg100 | 283 ± 184 | 444 ± 101 |

Early in training both networks *under*estimate (Q starts near 0 and needs hundreds of target updates to climb to ~100);
the overestimation appears once the values have propagated, and it is DQN's max over noisy estimates that pushes them past
the ceiling. Double DQN (online net picks the action, target net scores it) stays close to the truth, and on these seeds it
also solved faster and stayed more stable. The curves are `overestimation.dqn` / `overestimation.double_dqn` in the JSON.

## Episodes 16–17 — REINFORCE, baselines, actor-critic

Policy 4-64-64-2 (tanh) for all three; Adam 1e-3; γ = 0.99; no advantage normalisation (that would itself be a baseline).
REINFORCE updates after every episode; the baseline is a learned V(s) (4-64-64-1) fit to the returns; A2C uses 8 parallel
CartPoles × 16 steps per update with GAE (λ = 0.95). 500k env steps per run.

- **REINFORCE without a baseline: 3/5 seeds solved**, after 260k ± 53k
  steps when it did, and it rarely stays there (final avg100 229 ± 147).
- **With a learned baseline: 5/5, after 83k ± 20k
  steps** (~299 episodes, 1.1 s), and it stays solved
  (final 498 ± 3): about
  3× fewer steps than the seeds of plain REINFORCE that solved at all, and every seed.
- **A2C + GAE: 5/5, after 213k ± 53k steps**, and the most
  stable finish of all (every seed at 500 at the end). In env steps it was slower than REINFORCE + baseline with these
  settings (it takes small 128-sample steps); in wall time it was the fastest (1.9 s to solve).

**How much noise does a baseline remove?** (`logs/deep_gradvar.json`) We froze one policy and computed the policy-gradient
estimate from each of 300 fresh episodes three ways — A = G_t (no baseline), A = G_t − V(s_t), and GAE — on the very same
episodes, then measured the variance across episodes (the estimator is the textbook sum Σ_t A_t ∇log π(a_t|s_t); V is fit to
returns of 100 separate episodes).

| Policy (snapshot of the REINFORCE run, seed 10) | Mean episode length | Variance, no baseline ÷ baseline | ÷ GAE | One weight (push-right bias): no baseline ÷ baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| untrained (episode 0) | 23 | 2.7× | 5.9× | 1.1× |
| learning (episode 400, avg return 177) | 163 | **45×** | **101×** | 47× |
| good (episode 700, avg return 321) | 368 | 22× | 121× | 26× |

At the "learning" policy the averaged gradients of the two unbiased estimators point the same way (cosine
0.97) — the baseline keeps the signal and removes
~98% of the noise. Signal-to-noise per episode:
0.016 → 0.68 → 1.44.
(GAE trades a little bias for even less variance.)

## Episode 18 — PPO

Policy and value nets 4-64-64 (tanh), 8 envs × 64 steps = 512 samples per update, 10 epochs × 4 minibatches, Adam 3e-4,
clip ε = 0.2, GAE λ = 0.95, γ = 0.99, 300k steps. "No clip" is identical except the objective is ratio × advantage.

- **PPO solved in 5/5 seeds after 84k ± 24k steps**
  (~358 episodes, 2.5 s), and every seed stayed solved
  (final avg100 500 ± 0).
- **Clip fraction 3.7%** of samples per update (± 0.5 across seeds;
  4.1% while still learning); **approximate KL per update 0.0030**
  (exact KL old→new after the update 0.0044). The clip only bites on a few percent of samples,
  and that is enough.
- **Without the clip** the policy moves 35× further per update (approx KL
  0.105, 14% of ratios outside [0.8, 1.2], single updates up to KL ≈
  3.1). It still solved (5/5, after 107k ± 24k) but did not
  stay solved: final avg100 387 ± 154 vs 500 with the clip.
- **Reuse each batch harder (20 epochs, lr 1e-3)** and the gap widens: with the clip PPO solved in
  58k ± 6k steps (clip fraction
  6.6%, approx KL 0.0067); without it,
  234k ± 76k steps, with an average approx KL of
  2.7 per update: the policy lurches from update to update and needs
  4× as many steps.

## Episode 19 — Pendulum swing-up

SAC (CleanRL's settings: actor and two critics 256-256 ReLU, Adam 3e-4 / 1e-3, batch 256, τ = 0.005, automatic entropy
temperature, action = 2·tanh(u)), 20k env steps = 100 episodes, the first 1,000 steps random. Evaluation = deterministic
policy 2·tanh(mean) on the same 10 start states.

- **SAC final return -147.4 ± 1.6** (seeds -149.8 to -145.7),
  better than −150 on every seed. It first averaged above −200 after only 4,400 env steps (~22 episodes).
  Wall time 56 s per 20k-step run (≈ 2.8 ms per step: one critic and one actor update each).
- **PPO for contrast** (Gaussian policy, 4 envs × 512 steps, 10 epochs, lr 1e-3, γ = 0.9): -222 ± 22
  after 502k env steps (29 s); it first reached −200 after
  121k steps on average (28× SAC's
  4,400). SAC reaches a better pendulum with far fewer environment steps — replaying old data
  pays off when steps are expensive — while PPO's cheap updates get through steps much faster in wall time.

## Caveats

- **CartPole is easy.** A 4-number state, two actions and a dense reward: it is a teaching instrument, not a benchmark.
  Differences of tens of thousands of steps between methods here say little about Atari or robots; the robust findings
  are the ablations that fail outright (no replay, no target network) and the large effects (the 20–100× gradient-variance
  cut from a baseline, the 35× larger policy steps without the clip, SAC vs PPO sample counts on Pendulum).
- **Five seeds** give the ± values shown; with spreads this large (DQN: 118k steps) only big
  differences are meaningful. The DQN ranking vs Double DQN is consistent with the literature but not statistically strong.
- **Hyperparameters matter and were piloted.** DQN in particular needed a pilot sweep (configs A–J in `logs/pilot/`; the
  first configs solved few or none of their pilot seeds within 150k–300k steps). The reported config was then run on
  fresh seeds. PPO, REINFORCE, A2C and SAC used their first-choice settings (standard values from the PPO paper /
  SB3 / CleanRL); the "aggressive" PPO pair was added after a pilot showed the clip matters more there.
- **A bug we found and fixed:** value networks first fit raw returns (up to 100) and their tanh units saturated, turning
  V(s) into a constant. All critics now predict return/100 (`value_scale`). The superseded runs are in `logs/superseded/`
  (with the saturated, effectively constant critic: REINFORCE + baseline 5/5 solved, 129k steps; A2C 5/5 solved, 163k steps; PPO 5/5 solved, 116k steps —
  a constant baseline already helps a lot; the state-dependent one sped up REINFORCE and PPO, while A2C got a little slower).
- **Training-time normalisation:** REINFORCE's training loss averages over an episode's steps (common practice); the
  gradient-variance measurement uses the textbook sum so that the baseline is exactly unbiased.
- **"Solved" is measured on training episodes** (ε-greedy or sampled actions). Greedy evaluation of the final DQN
  network at 500k steps averaged 337; the exported DQN network is the best evaluated
  snapshot (see `weights/dqn_cartpole.json`).
- **Time limits:** at CartPole's 500-step cut-off the state is not terminal, so DQN, A2C and PPO bootstrap through it;
  REINFORCE uses plain Monte Carlo returns. Q-values are discounted (γ = 0.99), not episode lengths.
- **Wall times** come from runs sharing the machine (ten seeds at once, plus other agents' jobs).

## Files

- `deep_rl.json` — headline numbers, per-seed results, hyperparameters, curves (≤ 200 points; x in env steps and episodes).
- `weights/{dqn_cartpole, reinforce_cartpole, ppo_cartpole, sac_pendulum, random_cartpole}.npz` + `.json` — networks
